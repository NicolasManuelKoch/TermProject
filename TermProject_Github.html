Neuroplasticity

Refers to the lifelong reorganization of the brain's connections, structures and functions

Hippocampus

A region responsible for the brain's most important functions such as communication and computations.

Neurons

Cells in the nervous system that receive and relay information using chemical and electrical transmission.

Prefrontal cortex

A region in the brain, located just above the eyes and behind the forehead, important for planning and decision making (executive function)

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)

A form of psychotherapy that stresses the links between behaviour, thoughts and feelings.

Synaptic pruning

A process by which redundant neural connections are eliminated.

Neurogenesis

The "birth" of new neurons. This may even happen into old age, in the hippocampus and possibly olfactory bulb.

Episodic memory

Refers to longterm memory of experiences from ones own life.

Synapses

Connections between neurons. This is where neurotransmitters diffuse from one neuron to another to facilitate communication.

Primary visual cortex

A brain area located in the very "back" of the brain. Plasticity may be more limited here in certain areas after critical periods have passed.

Structural adaptation

Neural connections can be strengthened, weakened, refined, or reorganized over time. The architecture of the brain reflects ongoing interaction with the world.

Functional flexibility

Brain activity is not locked into one pattern forever. Networks can shift how they coordinate and distribute work as new demands, skills, and habits emerge.

Intentional change

Practice, attention, learning, and repeated engagement can all influence how patterns become more efficient, more stable, or more adaptable.
